If an alarm sounds

Do this now
  1. Get everyone outside into fresh air, including pets.
  2. Leave the door open on your way out.
  3. Call 911 from outside or a neighbor’s home.
  4. Stay out until the fire department says it is safe.

In the days after an exposure

  • See a doctor, even if you feel better, and say you suspect carbon monoxide.
  • Ask for the reports from the fire department and gas utility.
  • Keep the equipment. Don’t let anyone remove or repair it before it is inspected, if you can avoid it.
  • Write things down: who was affected, symptoms, times and who you spoke to.
  • Don’t sign anything from a landlord, hotel or insurer until you understand your rights.

The first few days decide what can be proved later.

Preventing exposure at home

  • Fit carbon monoxide alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas.
  • Service heating, water heaters and other fuel-burning appliances every year.
  • Have chimneys and flues checked and cleared each year.
  • Never run a generator indoors, in a garage, or within 20 feet of doors, windows or vents.
  • Never use a charcoal grill, camp stove or gas oven to heat a home or tent.
  • Never leave a car running in a garage, even with the door open.
  • Clear snow from vents, flues and car tailpipes after storms.

When you travel

Hotels, vacation rentals, cabins and RVs don’t always have alarms. A small portable carbon monoxide alarm in your luggage costs little and works anywhere there is a fuel-burning appliance nearby.
