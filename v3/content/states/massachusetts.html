The short answer

In brief

In Massachusetts you generally have three years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, and three years for a wrongful death claim, counted from the date of death or from when the family knew, or should have known, what caused it.

Under Nicole’s Law, homes with fuel-burning equipment or an attached garage must have working carbon monoxide alarms on every level. A missing or dead alarm can be strong evidence in a claim.

Massachusetts time limits

Personal injury: 3 yearsFrom when the claim arose (M.G.L. c. 260, § 2A).
Wrongful death: 3 yearsFrom the date of death, or from when the executor knew or should have known the cause (M.G.L. c. 229, § 2).
Public employers: special rulesA claim against a city, town or the state must first be presented in writing, and damages are generally capped at $100,000 (M.G.L. c. 258).
Evidence: much soonerAlarms, appliances and service records can be replaced within days. Keep them if you can.

Nicole’s Law: carbon monoxide alarms in Massachusetts

Nicole’s Law (M.G.L. c. 148, § 26F½) is named after Nicole Garofalo, a seven-year-old girl who died in January 2005 when snow blocked a heating vent at her family’s home and carbon monoxide built up inside. Since 2006 the law has required carbon monoxide alarms in most residences that have fossil-fuel-burning equipment, such as a furnace, boiler, water heater or fireplace, or that have enclosed parking or an attached garage.

Alarms must be installed on every level of the home, including habitable parts of basements and attics. The detailed rules are in 527 CMR 31, and a home must pass a smoke and carbon monoxide alarm inspection before it can be sold. Tenants may not disable or remove required alarms (M.G.L. c. 148, § 27A), and landlords must provide them.

A vent blocked by snow. That is how Nicole died.

What we see in Massachusetts

Snow blocking heating and dryer vents after storms, oil and gas boilers in older multi-family houses, triple-deckers with shared heating, attached garages with cars or snow blowers left running, and generators used during winter power cuts.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or ownerRequired alarms, safe heating and venting, and repairs once a problem is reported.
Contractors and oil or gas companiesInstalled or serviced the boiler, furnace or vent.
ManufacturersMade equipment or alarms that failed or lacked warnings.
Public housing and citiesSame duties, with special claim rules and limits on damages.

Questions

Does my Massachusetts apartment need a carbon monoxide alarm?

If the building has fuel-burning heat, a gas water heater or stove, a fireplace, or an attached garage, almost certainly yes, on every level of your home. Ask the landlord in writing if it is missing.

How long do I have to file a carbon monoxide claim in Massachusetts?

Generally three years for an injury and three years for a wrongful death claim. Claims against a city, town or the state have extra steps, so speak to a lawyer early.
