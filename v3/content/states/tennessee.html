The short answer

In brief

In Tennessee you generally have only one year to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, and wrongful death claims are generally subject to one year as well. If criminal charges are brought against the person who caused the harm, the deadline can be two years. Claims against a city, county or other local government must be filed within twelve months.

Tennessee hotels with gas heaters, fuel-burning appliances or fireplaces must have a carbon monoxide alarm within ten feet of every sleeping room, and keep it working.

Tennessee time limits

Personal injury: 1 yearFrom when the claim arose (Tenn. Code Ann. 28-3-104(a)(1)).
With criminal charges: 2 yearsWhen criminal charges are brought against the person alleged to have caused the injury (28-3-104(a)(2)).
Wrongful death: generally 1 yearUsually counted from the date of death. Ask a lawyer straight away.
City or county: 12 monthsUnder the Governmental Tort Liability Act (Tenn. Code Ann. 29-20-305(b)).

Carbon monoxide alarms in Tennessee

Tennessee law (Tenn. Code Ann. 68-120-112) requires the owner or operator of a hotel that has a fossil-fuel-burning heater or appliance, a fireplace, or another feature that can give off carbon monoxide to install an approved carbon monoxide alarm within ten feet of each room used for sleeping. The owner or manager must keep every required alarm working, with the maintenance, repairs and tests that takes.

A rented or leased recreational vehicle must have a working carbon monoxide detector (Tenn. Code Ann. 55-9-218). For houses and apartments, carbon monoxide alarm rules come mainly from the building codes adopted locally and apply mostly to newer construction, so many older rentals have none. Landlords still have to keep heating and appliances safe and repair problems once they are reported.

One year passes quickly when you are still recovering.

What we see in Tennessee

Hotels and cabin rentals with gas fireplaces, pool heaters and boilers near guest rooms, gas furnaces and water heaters in older apartments, space heaters in cold snaps, and generators run in garages or close to windows after storms knock out the power.

Questions

How long do I have to file a carbon monoxide claim in Tennessee?

In most cases one year. It can be two years if criminal charges are brought against the person who caused the injury, and twelve months applies to claims against a city or county. Because the time is so short, get advice as soon as you can.

I was poisoned in a Tennessee hotel or cabin. What should I do?

Get medical care first and keep the records, including any blood test for carbon monoxide. Write down the room number and what you noticed, and ask whether the fire department took a reading. Then call us for a free review.
