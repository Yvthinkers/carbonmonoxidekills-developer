The short answer

In brief

In Georgia you generally have two years to file a personal injury or wrongful death claim for carbon monoxide poisoning. If a city may be responsible, for example through public housing or a city building, written “ante litem” notice is due within six months. For a county or the state it is twelve months.

Whether a carbon monoxide alarm was required depends mostly on when the building was built and the codes in force then, so the case usually turns on whether the heating and venting were safe.

Georgia time limits

Injury and wrongful death: 2 yearsFrom the poisoning, or from the date of death (O.C.G.A. 9-3-33).
City: 6 monthsWritten ante litem notice to the city (O.C.G.A. 36-33-5).
County: 12 monthsWritten notice of the claim (O.C.G.A. 36-11-1).
State: 12 monthsNotice under the Georgia Tort Claims Act (O.C.G.A. 50-21-26).

Carbon monoxide alarms in Georgia

Georgia law has long required smoke detectors in homes, apartments, hotels and dormitories (O.C.G.A. 25-2-40). Carbon monoxide alarm requirements come mainly from the building and fire codes the state adopts, and they apply mostly to newer buildings. An older home or rental may not have needed one.

That does not end a claim. Landlords, hotels and contractors still have to keep furnaces, water heaters, gas logs and pool heaters installed, vented and maintained safely.

Notice to a city within six months. Most people have never heard of it.

What we see in Georgia

Gas furnaces and water heaters in older apartments, gas logs and space heaters during cold snaps, hotels with boilers or pool heaters near rooms, and generators used after storms and hurricanes. Many homes have no alarm at all.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or ownerSafe heating and venting, and repairs once a problem is reported.
HotelsOwe guests reasonably safe rooms, including equipment nearby.
Housing authorities and citiesSame duties, with the short six-month notice.
Contractors and manufacturersInstalled, serviced or made the equipment that failed.

Questions

I live in public housing in Georgia. Is there a shorter deadline?

Very possibly. If the city is responsible, written notice is generally due within six months. Speak to a lawyer quickly.

There was no alarm. Can I still claim?

Often, yes. The question is whether the heating or appliance was installed and maintained safely, and whether the owner knew or should have known about the danger.
