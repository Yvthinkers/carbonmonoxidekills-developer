The short answer

In brief

In West Virginia you generally have two years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, and two years from the date of death for a wrongful death claim. Claims against a city, county or other local government body also have two years, with their own special rules.

West Virginia law requires carbon monoxide detectors in newer homes with fuel-burning heating or cooking, and in schools and daycare centers that use fuel-burning heat. Even where no detector was required, the owner still has to keep heating and appliances safe.

West Virginia time limits

Personal injury: 2 yearsUsually from the poisoning, or from when the injury was discovered (W. Va. Code 55-2-12).
Wrongful death: 2 yearsFrom the date of death (W. Va. Code 55-7-6).
City or county: 2 yearsClaims against a political subdivision, under the Governmental Tort Claims and Insurance Reform Act (W. Va. Code 29-12A-6).
Young children: longerFor a child under ten, a claim against a local government body can be brought until the child’s twelfth birthday if that is later (29-12A-6).

Carbon monoxide detectors in West Virginia

West Virginia’s smoke and carbon monoxide detector law (W. Va. Code 29-3-16a) requires a carbon monoxide detector in newly built residential units that have fuel-burning heating or cooking, and in public and private schools and daycare centers that use fuel-burning heating. Whoever installs a detector must explain to the occupants the dangers of carbon monoxide and how the detector works.

Many older homes and rentals were built before these rules and may have no detector. That does not end a claim. Landlords, contractors and gas companies still have to install, vent and maintain furnaces, boilers, water heaters and stoves safely, and to fix problems once they are reported.

The detector is the only warning. Older homes often don’t have one.

What we see in West Virginia

Gas furnaces and boilers in older houses and apartments, space heaters and blocked flues in cold weather, mobile homes with heaters in tight spaces, and portable generators run too close to the house after storms and flooding cut the power. Workplace exposures happen too, in garages, warehouses and enclosed work sites.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or ownerSafe heating and venting, required detectors, and repairs once a problem is reported.
Contractors and installersInstalled or serviced the furnace, boiler, water heater or flue.
ManufacturersMade a heater, generator or detector that failed or lacked proper warnings.
Cities, counties and schoolsSame duties for buildings they run, with special rules for claims against them.

Questions

How long do I have to file a carbon monoxide claim in West Virginia?

Generally two years from the poisoning, or two years from the date of death in a wrongful death case. The start date can be different if the injury was only discovered later, so speak to a lawyer early.

There was no carbon monoxide detector. Can I still claim?

Often, yes. A missing detector can matter, but most cases turn on why the carbon monoxide was there: a faulty appliance, a blocked or broken flue, or poor maintenance by the owner or a contractor.
