The short answer

In brief

In California you generally have two years to file a personal injury or wrongful death claim for carbon monoxide poisoning. If a city, county, school district or state agency may be responsible, a written government claim is usually due within six months, long before the lawsuit deadline.

California law requires carbon monoxide alarms in most homes and hotel rooms that have a fuel-burning appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage. Whether one was fitted, and whether it worked, is one of the first things to check.

California time limits

Injury: 2 yearsFrom the date of the poisoning, in most cases (Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1).
Wrongful death: 2 yearsCounted from the date of death (§ 335.1).
Government body: 6 monthsA written claim to the public entity before any lawsuit (Government Code § 911.2).
ExceptionsChildren, and injuries discovered later, can follow different rules. Have a lawyer confirm your date.

California’s carbon monoxide alarm law

The Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Prevention Act of 2010 (SB 183) requires the owner of a dwelling unit that has a fossil-fuel heater or appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage to install a carbon monoxide device approved by the State Fire Marshal (Health and Safety Code § 17926).

  • Single-family homes: required since July 1, 2011.
  • Apartments and all other dwelling units: required since January 1, 2013.
  • Hotel and motel rooms: required since January 1, 2017.
  • Landlords may enter a rental to install, repair and test the device. A tenant should report a missing or faulty alarm in writing.

The law has required alarms in most California homes since 2013. A missing alarm is evidence.

What we see in California

Older apartment buildings with wall furnaces and shared boiler rooms, hotels and vacation rentals with pool heaters, and portable generators run during power shutoffs. Gas appliances in tightly sealed homes, and cars left running in attached garages, account for many of the rest.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or ownerMust provide a habitable home and the alarms the law requires.
Hotels and vacation rentalsRequired to fit alarms in rooms with a carbon monoxide source since 2017.
Heating and gas contractorsInstalled, serviced or “fixed” the equipment that failed.
ManufacturersAppliances, generators or alarms that were defective.

Questions

My rental had no carbon monoxide alarm. Was one required?

If the home has a gas appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage, very likely yes. California has required alarms in apartments and other rentals since January 1, 2013.

I was poisoned in a California hotel. What should I do first?

Get medical care and ask for a carboxyhemoglobin (COHb) blood test. Then write down your room number and keep your booking, receipts and any photos. Hotel and motel rooms have needed alarms since 2017.

How long do I have in California?

Usually two years from the poisoning, or from the date of death for a family. A claim against a government body needs a written claim within six months.
