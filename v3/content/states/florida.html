The short answer

In brief

For carbon monoxide poisoning that happened after March 24, 2023, Florida generally gives you two years to file a negligence claim. Before that date the limit was four years. A wrongful death claim must also be filed within two years. Claims against the state or a local government have their own written-notice step first.

Florida’s alarm law, Janelle’s Law, covers newer buildings. Most Florida cases we see involve generators, boats and fuel-burning appliances.

Florida time limits

Injury: 2 yearsFor negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023 (Florida Statutes § 95.11(5)(a)). Four years for older ones.
Wrongful death: 2 yearsCounted from the date of death (§ 95.11(5)(e)).
Government bodyWritten notice to the agency, and, unless it is a city or county, to the Department of Financial Services, within 3 years (§ 768.28(6)).
ExceptionsSome product and medical claims follow different rules. Have a lawyer confirm your date.

Janelle’s Law: carbon monoxide alarms in Florida

Florida Statutes § 553.885 requires an approved, working carbon monoxide alarm within 10 feet of every room used for sleeping in new buildings and additions that have a fossil-fuel heater or appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage. It applies to buildings and additions constructed on or after July 1, 2008. Hospitals, inpatient hospices and nursing homes follow separate rules. Battery, hard-wired and combined smoke and carbon monoxide alarms all qualify.

Older homes and rentals may not have been required to have an alarm under state law. The owner can still be responsible for heating, venting or generator use that made the home unsafe.

An alarm within 10 feet of every bedroom. In any building constructed since July 2008.

What we see in Florida

  • Portable generators after hurricanes, run in garages, on porches or under open windows.
  • Boats: exhaust and generator fumes collecting at the stern, in cabins or at swim platforms.
  • Cars left running in attached garages, including keyless cars that were never switched off.
  • Hotels, condos and vacation rentals with pool heaters, boilers or garages beneath rooms.

Who may be responsible

Landlord, condo or hotel ownerSafe premises, safe equipment and the alarms the law requires.
ManufacturersGenerators, boats and appliances that were defective or lacked warnings.
Boat operators and rental companiesExhaust systems and safe use of generators on board.
ContractorsInstalled or serviced the appliance or venting that failed.

Questions

I was poisoned before March 2023. Which deadline applies?

Claims that arose before March 24, 2023 generally keep the old four-year limit. Claims after that date generally have two years. Ask a lawyer to check your exact date.

A neighbor’s generator made us ill. Is that a claim?

It can be. Whoever ran the generator, and sometimes the manufacturer, may be responsible. Keep photos of where it was placed and any fire department report.
