The short answer

In brief

In New York you generally have three years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, and two years from the date of death for a wrongful death claim. If a city, county, town, public housing authority or other public body may be responsible, a notice of claim is usually due within 90 days.

Under Amanda’s Law, one- and two-family homes, condos, co-ops and apartment buildings with a fuel-burning appliance or an attached garage must have a working carbon monoxide alarm.

New York time limits

Injury: 3 yearsFrom the date of the poisoning, in most cases (CPLR § 214(5)).
Wrongful death: 2 yearsCounted from the date of death (Estates, Powers and Trusts Law § 5-4.1).
Public body: 90 daysNotice of claim against a city, county, town or public authority (General Municipal Law § 50-e). The lawsuit itself usually follows within a year and 90 days.
ExceptionsChildren and some other cases follow different rules. Have a lawyer confirm your date.

Amanda’s Law

Amanda Hansen was 16 when she died of carbon monoxide poisoning from a defective boiler while sleeping over at a friend’s home in West Seneca, New York, in January 2009. The law that followed, Executive Law § 378(5-a), requires an operable carbon monoxide alarm in:

  • Every one- and two-family home, whatever its age and whether or not it has been sold.
  • Condominium and co-op units.
  • Multiple dwellings: buildings where three or more families live independently, including rental apartments.
  • Only where it is needed: homes with appliances or systems that can give off carbon monoxide, or an attached garage.

New York City adds its own rules for apartment owners and tenants on top of the state law.

A boiler, a sleepover, a 16-year-old. That is why the law exists.

What we see in New York

Old boilers and furnaces in basements, flues shared by several apartments, gas stoves used for heat, and generators and cars during winter storms and outages. In apartment buildings carbon monoxide can travel through walls, shafts and stairwells, so neighbors who are ill at the same time is an important clue.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or building ownerBoilers, venting and the alarms Amanda’s Law requires.
Managing agent or superDay-to-day maintenance and response to complaints.
Public housing authoritiesSame duties, but with the short 90-day notice deadline.
Contractors and manufacturersInstalled, serviced or made the equipment that failed.

Questions

I live in public housing. Does the 90-day rule apply to me?

Very likely. Claims against a public housing authority or a city usually need a notice of claim within 90 days. Get advice quickly.

Our building had no carbon monoxide alarm. Was one required?

If the building has a boiler, furnace, gas appliance or attached garage, Amanda’s Law very likely required one. Photograph where alarms are, or should be.
