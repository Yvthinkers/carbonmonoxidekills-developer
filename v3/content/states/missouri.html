The short answer

In brief

In Missouri you generally have five years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning. A wrongful death claim must be filed within three years of the date of death.

Missouri has no statewide law requiring carbon monoxide alarms in existing homes, so the rules depend on your city or county code. Even without an alarm rule, landlords, contractors and manufacturers still have to keep heating and appliances safe.

Carbon monoxide alarms in Missouri

Unlike most states, Missouri has no statewide statute requiring carbon monoxide alarms in houses or apartments. Requirements come from the building and fire codes adopted by individual cities and counties, which often cover new construction and some rentals, so whether an alarm was required depends on where you live and when the building was built.

A missing alarm is not the end of a claim. Most cases turn on why the carbon monoxide was there: a cracked heat exchanger, a blocked or disconnected flue, a water heater installed without proper venting, or a generator or vehicle running too close to living space.

No statewide alarm law means many Missouri homes have none.

What we see in Missouri

Gas furnaces and water heaters in older rental houses and apartments in Kansas City, St. Louis and Springfield, space heaters in cold snaps, attached garages, and generators run during ice storms and power cuts. Hotels and motels with boilers and pool heaters near guest rooms are a risk too.

Questions

How long do I have to file a carbon monoxide claim in Missouri?

Generally five years for an injury. If someone died, the family generally has three years from the date of death. Don’t rely on the full time: evidence and witnesses are easier to find early.

My rental had no carbon monoxide alarm. Can I still claim?

Often, yes. Whether an alarm was required depends on your local code, but the landlord still had to keep the heating and appliances safe. The cause of the leak is usually the key question.
