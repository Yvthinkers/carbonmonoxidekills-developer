Carbon monoxide lawyer in Illinois: an alarm within 15 feet of every bedroom.

Since 2007 Illinois has required a working carbon monoxide alarm within 15 feet of every room used for sleeping, in homes with fuel-burning heat or appliances or an attached garage.

The short answer

In brief

In Illinois you generally have two years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, and two years from the date of death for a wrongful death claim. Claims against a city, county, park district or other local public body usually have to be filed within one year.

The Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act requires a working alarm within 15 feet of every bedroom in homes and apartments with a fossil-fuel heater or appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage. The owner must supply and install it.

Illinois time limits

Injury: 2 yearsFrom the date of the poisoning, in most cases (735 ILCS 5/13-202).
Wrongful death: 2 yearsCounted from the date of death (Wrongful Death Act, 740 ILCS 180/2).
Local public body: 1 yearLawsuits against local governments and their employees (Tort Immunity Act, 745 ILCS 10/8-101).
ExceptionsChildren and injuries discovered later can follow different rules. Have a lawyer confirm your date.

The Illinois Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act

  • Where: at least one approved, working alarm within 15 feet of every room used for sleeping (430 ILCS 135/10).
  • Which homes: dwelling units with a fossil-fuel heater or appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage, including apartments.
  • Who: the owner supplies and installs the alarm. During a lease the tenant usually tests it and changes the batteries.
  • Which alarms: battery, plug-in with battery backup, or hard-wired with battery backup, tested to the UL 2034 standard.

Willfully failing to install or maintain a required alarm is a Class B misdemeanor. Cities, including Chicago, can and do add their own rules.

Within 15 feet of every bedroom. Measure it.

What we see in Illinois

Long, cold winters mean furnaces and boilers run for months, often in older two-flats and apartment buildings where flues are shared. Blocked chimneys after storms, generators during outages and cars warmed up in attached garages are the other common sources.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or ownerSafe heating and venting, and the alarms the Act requires.
Property managersMaintenance, inspections and response to complaints.
Heating contractors and utilitiesInstalled, serviced or inspected the system that failed.
ManufacturersFurnaces, heaters, generators or alarms that were defective.

Questions

The alarm in my apartment was more than 15 feet from the bedroom. Does that matter?

It can. Illinois law sets the distance. Photograph and measure where the alarm was, and keep your lease.

The building is owned by a housing authority. How long do I have?

Claims against a local public body generally have to be filed within one year, much sooner than the usual two. Get advice early.

Will I be working with an Illinois lawyer?

Yes. After a free review, Rob’s team refers cases to partner attorneys licensed in Illinois. Carbon Monoxide Kills is not a law firm itself.
