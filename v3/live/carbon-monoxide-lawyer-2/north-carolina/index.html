Carbon monoxide lawyer in North Carolina: one alarm per level, new batteries at move-in.

North Carolina landlords must provide a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of a rental with fuel-burning heat, appliances, a fireplace or an attached garage, and fix a reported fault within 15 days.

The short answer

In brief

In North Carolina you generally have three years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, but only two years from the date of death for a wrongful death claim. Families should not assume they have the longer period.

Landlords must provide at least one working carbon monoxide alarm per level in rentals with a fossil-fuel heater, appliance or fireplace, or an attached garage, check it at the start of each tenancy and fit new batteries.

North Carolina time limits

Injury: 3 yearsFrom the date of the poisoning, in most cases (G.S. 1-52(16)).
Wrongful death: 2 yearsCounted from the date of death (G.S. 1-53(4)).
Government bodiesClaims against the state go to the Industrial Commission under the State Tort Claims Act, with their own rules.
ExceptionsChildren and injuries discovered later can follow different rules. Have a lawyer confirm your date.

Landlords’ carbon monoxide duties

Under G.S. 42-42(a)(7), in force since the end of 2012, a landlord of a home with a fossil-fuel heater, appliance or fireplace, or an attached garage, must:

  • Provide at least one working carbon monoxide alarm per level of each rental unit.
  • Check it at the start of every tenancy, and fit new batteries in battery alarms unless the lease says otherwise.
  • Repair or replace it within 15 days of written notice from the tenant.

Tenants change batteries during the tenancy. A tenant not changing a battery is not, by itself, negligence by the tenant or the landlord. Vacation rentals have their own alarm rules under the Vacation Rental Act (G.S. 42A-31).

Reported in writing? The landlord had 15 days.

What we see in North Carolina

In 2013 three guests died of carbon monoxide in the same room of a hotel in Boone, after exhaust from a pool heater below leaked into it. Hotels, mountain cabins and beach vacation rentals remain a large share of North Carolina cases, along with furnaces and gas logs in rented homes, and generators after hurricanes and ice storms.

Who may be responsible

Landlord or ownerThe alarm duties above, and safe heating and venting.
Hotels and vacation rental ownersPool heaters, boilers and gas logs near guest rooms.
Rental management companiesMaintenance and inspections for vacation homes.
Contractors and manufacturersInstalled, serviced or made the equipment that failed.

Questions

I told my landlord the alarm wasn’t working. What happens now?

If you told them in writing, North Carolina law gives the landlord 15 days to repair or replace it. Keep a copy of your message and the date.

My father died from carbon monoxide. Do we have three years?

No. A wrongful death claim in North Carolina generally has to be filed within two years of the death.
