Carbon monoxide lawyer in Delaware: two years to act.

Most Delaware carbon monoxide claims must be filed within two years. Delaware also requires carbon monoxide detectors in rentals and lodging with fuel-burning equipment or an attached garage.

The short answer

In brief

In Delaware you generally have two years to file a personal injury claim for carbon monoxide poisoning, and two years for a wrongful death claim.

Delaware law requires the owner of a rental or lodging unit with a fuel-burning heater or appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage to install and maintain carbon monoxide detectors. A missing detector can be important evidence.

Delaware time limits

Personal injury: 2 yearsUsually from the date of the injury (10 Del. C. § 8119).
Wrongful death: 2 years10 Del. C. § 8107.
Government bodies: special rulesClaims against a city, county or the state can need early written notice. Get advice quickly.
Evidence: much soonerDetectors and appliances get replaced within days. Keep them if you can.

Carbon monoxide detectors in Delaware

Delaware’s carbon monoxide detection law (16 Del. C. ch. 66C) requires the owner of a lodging establishment, including rental units, to install carbon monoxide detectors where a unit has a fossil-fuel-burning heater or appliance, a fireplace or another feature that can produce carbon monoxide, or an attached garage. Combination smoke and carbon monoxide alarms count.

The owner must install and maintain the detectors. On a lease of a month or longer, the tenant is responsible for keeping the battery working. According to the State Fire Marshal, existing buildings had to comply by the end of 2020.

The law puts the detector on the owner.

What we see in Delaware

Gas furnaces and water heaters in older apartments, beach rentals and hotels in Rehoboth, Dewey and Bethany with boilers or pool heaters, attached garages, and generators used during coastal storms and power cuts.

Who may be responsible

Landlords and rental ownersRequired detectors, safe heating and venting, and repairs once a problem is reported.
Hotels and vacation rentalsRequired detectors, and safe rooms and equipment for guests.
Contractors and manufacturersInstalled, serviced or made the equipment that failed.
Public bodiesSame duties for buildings they run, with special claim rules.

Questions

Does my Delaware rental need a carbon monoxide detector?

If the unit has a gas or oil heater or appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage, the owner generally must install and maintain one. Ask the landlord in writing if it is missing.

How long do I have to file a carbon monoxide claim in Delaware?

Generally two years, for both injury and wrongful death claims. Claims against government bodies can need notice much sooner.
