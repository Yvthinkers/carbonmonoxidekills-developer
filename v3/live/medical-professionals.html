For medical professionals

Suspected carbon monoxide poisoning? We can point you to an expert.

In general practice it is hard to keep up with every subject. Our experts have dedicated their working lives to carbon monoxide and its physiological, neurological and cognitive effects.

Clinical reminders

  • Ask about the setting. Several people or pets ill at once, symptoms that ease away from home, or worse when heating runs.
  • Test early. COHb falls quickly with fresh air or oxygen. Record the time of the sample and of the last exposure.
  • Don’t rely on pulse oximetry. Standard SpO₂ readings can look normal in significant poisoning.
  • Plan follow-up. Delayed neurological sequelae can appear days to weeks after apparent recovery.
Our medical expert to confirm David G. Penney, Ph.D. Professor of Physiology and Occupational & Environmental Health, and consultant on carbon monoxide poisoning. Dr. Penney has been an expert witness in many carbon monoxide cases and has advised the World Health Organization on the lower limits of indoor carbon monoxide.

If you specialize in carbon monoxide yourself, contact us. We are happy to share your details with other medical professionals.
